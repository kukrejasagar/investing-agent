## 1. Executive Snapshot

**Summary.** Seven months in, the oil shock has moved from an acute closure to a chronic, contested-flow phase. Gulf crude exports recovered sharply in September. Kpler shows regional exports above pre-war levels on 14 days of the month. Most of that recovery runs through bypass routes (Saudi Arabia's Red Sea port of Yanbu, the UAE's Fujairah) and shuttle tankers, not normal Hormuz traffic. Attacks on shipping have risen in step with traffic: UKMTO logged 16 attacks in ~10 days, and the IRGC struck a tanker on 9 Oct. US–Iran talks are stalled, and a US naval blockade has cut Iranian crude loadings to roughly zero. Brent is back above $100, inventories are down ~0.5 bn bbl since February, and diesel and jet fuel are the tightest part of the market. Inflation has turned back up, and the Fed and ECB both raised rates in September. Equities are near record highs, so markets are pricing a long, contained disruption rather than escalation.

### 6.2 Geopolitical & security spillovers

- 🔴 **US–Iran talks stalled.** On 8 Oct Iran's president called negotiations "meaningless". The US says it will not resume strikes before the midterms (3 Nov), while saying the war will end "one way or the other".
- 🔴 **Rising maritime attacks in the Gulf** as traffic recovers. More ships means more targets.
- 🟠 **Houthi control of the Bab el-Mandeb coast** gives Iran's network leverage over both chokepoints at once.
- 🟠 **Iraq-based militias** attacked Saudi infrastructure (E-W pipeline, 11 Sep). Iraq closed its border and opened an investigation.
- 🟡 **Pakistan-led mediation.** The UN Secretary-General endorsed it in Islamabad on 6 Oct.

### 6.3 Market reactions

- **Equities:** Records despite the shock, helped by AI-led earnings. Energy is outperforming; airlines and chemicals are lagging. A risk of complacency [J].
- **Bonds:** The global sell-off pushed the US 10-yr to a 24-year high. Rate hikes are being repriced in the US and euro area.
- **Commodities:** Brent and middle distillates lead; gold is above $4,000; food is at a 3-year-plus high.

### What would make it worse 🔺
- Another successful strike on the **East-West pipeline** or Yanbu (removes ~4–5 mb/d of bypass exports)
- Houthi **closure of Bab el-Mandeb**, which traps Yanbu exports
- Resumption of US strikes after the midterms, or Iranian strikes on Gulf export terminals (Ras Tanura, Fujairah)
- Insurance **cover withdrawn** for Gulf transits
- An early cold winter in Europe or Northeast Asia while Qatar LNG is offline

### What would make it better 🔻
- A written US–Iran framework or ceasefire with verifiable maritime terms
- The US easing the blockade in exchange for Hormuz guarantees (Iran has signalled it would reopen the strait on that basis)
- Faster IEA releases plus the extra G7 100 mb tranche
- More demand destruction (IEA: −2.5 mb/d in 2026)
